02 / ABOUT

A technical background built in the real world.

I'm Tyler, currently serving in the British Army's Royal Corps of Signals and transitioning into civilian life.

My background is in communications and IT systems, technical troubleshooting and working with technology in environments where understanding the problem matters just as much as knowing which button to press.

Outside of work, I build things. Websites, game systems, modifications, technical projects and the occasional idea that becomes substantially more complicated than originally intended.

I'm particularly interested in the intersection between technology, systems and education.

03

Experience.

BRITISH ARMY

MILITARY → CIVILIAN

COMMUNICATIONS / IT SYSTEMS / TECHNICAL SUPPORT

I've spent my Army career around communications, IT and the inevitable technical problems that come with them. That has meant learning systems properly rather than simply learning which buttons to press, particularly when something stops working and the answer isn't immediately obvious.

Troubleshooting has consequently become one of the things I'm most comfortable doing: breaking a problem down, testing assumptions and finding a practical solution.

I've also spent time supporting others, explaining technical subjects and delivering instruction. As I move into civilian life, I'm looking for work where that mixture of systems knowledge, problem-solving and communication remains useful.

05 / AWAY FROM THE KEYBOARD

There's more to life than work.

Warhammer 40,000 has occupied an increasingly difficult-to-justify amount of shelf space over the years. I've collected several armies and particularly enjoy the tactical and list-building side of the game, even if every finished army seems to create an entirely rational requirement for another one.

Airsoft is another longstanding interest, particularly milsim-style events and the combination of teamwork, communications and equipment that comes with them. The former Signals bloke taking an excessive interest in radios and load carriage probably isn't especially surprising.

Gaming has been a constant for me for years. My tastes lean heavily towards tactical shooters, military simulation and strategy, with Arma, Squad, Rainbow Six and DayZ all being longstanding favourites. That said, I'm just as happy disappearing into an RPG, a Souls game or whichever management game has most recently convinced me that staring at menus constitutes entertainment.

And at the slightly healthier end of the spectrum, I've recently started playing football around Bristol. I normally play in defence and am gradually discovering that being a competent left back involves rather more running than it appears to from the sofa.
